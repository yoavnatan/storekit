## What we're building
Multi-vendor internet mall for the Israeli market. Sellers open stores; shoppers discover across them. Two independent SEO surfaces: platform (discovery) + each store (standalone feel).

**Vision:** Enable anyone to open an online store easily and efficiently — one that generates profit, is fair, and is fully transparent.

**Mission (north star):** Build a Hebrew-language digital shopping mall that removes the technological and financial barriers for small businesses through built-in automation, a clean minimalist management interface, and a fair, transparent pricing model. (Payment/pricing specifics — subscription tiers + split checkout — in *Business model* below.)

**Business model:** Hebrew digital mall. Buyer can pay **either** per-store separately **or** one unified charge for the whole cart (decided 2026-07-22 — both offered; unified needs the processor to split one charge across multiple sub-merchants, and SUMIT's `multivendorcharge` proves it is buildable — GO_LIVE §3). **Pricing lives in `src/lib/pricing.ts`, the single source (since 2026-07-27)** — subscription tiers = fixed monthly fee + per-sale commission, higher tier = lower commission, charged **additively, never offset**; fees final, percentages still placeholder. Commission is **per-seller-account** (`Seller.tier?`), never a global config number — `store.config.ts` deliberately has no `commissionPercent`. **The monthly fee starts at the seller's FIRST SALE, capped at 2 months from signup — no time-boxed trial**; card taken at signup either way. The tier table, the invariant every number change must keep (`tests/pricing.test.ts` asserts it), why a trial was rejected, and the seller-facing wording that follows are all in `pricing.ts`'s header; the economics and what was rejected are in memory `project_business_model_pricing`. **Advertising is a fully separate component** — pay-per-actual-spend via Authorize/Capture (+ a disclosed platform margin), never offset against subscription/commission. **Sellers = registered businesses only** (עצמאים/עוסק פטור/מורשה), not private individuals — it matches the provider's sub-merchant requirement and separates us from the Israeli P2P marketplaces (My Product, Yad2), which serve private sellers via manual escrow rather than a branded storefront. **Positioning in COPY (user, 2026-07-28):** the pitch is the mall, never store-building software — "open a store in minutes / no code / free to start" is the Shopify script, and repeating it enters a comparison this platform loses. Lead with what a seller cannot get elsewhere: an Israeli digital mall, a store of your own, the pull of a mall, ads/SEO/shipping built in. Buyer-facing surfaces must never describe shoppers as something delivered to a seller — the homepage is a buyer's page first. **Fixed platform template only** — no per-seller site/theme design tooling, ever. **Zero-touch self-service** — no manual owner involvement in any flow (registration, upload, fulfillment, messaging, disputes); a feature that needs admin gatekeeping must default to an automated equivalent instead.

**Target segment:** catalog stores with **dozens of products**, not 5–10-item boutiques. This fits the whole design — real catalog management (bulk tools) becomes necessary not optional, a large SEO surface (many indexable product pages) makes the discovery bet pay off, and product-first clean UX serves browsing/search/categories rather than the bespoke atmosphere a tiny boutique wants. "Clean & fast" reads as professional for a catalog, not "cheap" — the over-designed boutique is the wrong mirror to judge against.

### Payment architecture — decided (2026-07-06)
**Split payment via the payment processor.** Platform never holds seller funds — each seller has a sub-merchant account with the processor, which splits payment automatically at checkout (seller's share → seller, commission → platform). Reason: platform holding + manually disbursing seller money would be a regulated PSP service in Israel; split payment keeps the platform a pure software/marketplace layer.
- **Buyer pays per-store OR unified (decided 2026-07-22 — offer both).** Per-store = one charge per seller (independent refunds/cancellations, today's cart UI). Unified = one card charge for the whole cart, split by the processor across every seller's sub-merchant account.
- **Provider not committed — PayMe leads, SUMIT second, and תקבול (Takbull) is OUT (reviewed 2026-08-03: its marketplace product is a WooCommerce plugin, don't propose it again).** The comparison, the regulatory frame that fixes this architecture, and the open questions per provider are in `GO_LIVE_CHECKLIST.md` §3 — a one-time setup decision, not a per-session rule.
- No manual payout queue — the processor pays sellers directly, so admin never marks a payout "paid". `SellerBalance` and the admin dashboard are **reporting** views only.
- Commission is deducted at processor/order level, at the rate the seller's own tier gives (`lib/pricing.ts`) — **not** from a global config value; `store.config.ts` deliberately has no `commissionPercent`.

## Architecture
- **Stack:** Astro 7 + Node adapter, TypeScript, Tailwind v4, self-hosted Heebo, `tokens.css` color system. Dev toolbar disabled. Astro 7's compiler rejects an HTML comment as the first child right after `{expr && (` — place comments above the expression, not inside it. **In a page this fails SILENTLY in dev:** the route stops building, so a static route falls through to a dynamic sibling — `/stores` started 302-ing to `/404` via `[storeSlug]` with no error anywhere, and `astro check` still reported 0 errors. A page that suddenly 404s/redirects after an edit is this, not a routing bug.
- **Fonts:** self-hosted Heebo — hand-written `@font-face` in `main.css`, `font-display:optional` (never `swap`), and all 10 weight×script combos preloaded in `BaseLayout.astro`. Both files carry the measurements behind those choices; don't drop a preload without re-measuring layout shift.
- **View Transitions — investigated, not adopted (2026-07-16), and not safe to retry without a fresh check:** `ClientRouter` is opt-out-per-link, so enabling it soft-navigates the whole site by default; the JS-free native `@view-transition` was tried and reverted the same session. Memory `project_view_transitions_font_blocker`.
- **Prefetch:** Astro's built-in `prefetch`, `prefetchAll:false` + hover — opt in per link with `data-astro-prefetch`, never site-wide. Its one real limit (links built later via innerHTML can't use it) is in `astro.config.mjs`. Back still beats any prefetch: bfcache restores with zero work.
- **Split:** content pages = static, seller/admin/api = SSR.
- **Header:** `position:fixed`; **height is pinned INLINE on `<header>`** (`height:3.4rem;box-sizing:border-box`) + `body{padding-top:3.4rem}` — never sticky, and the height must NEVER change (fixed inline so it can't flash-then-shrink before CSS/fonts load — the dev server injects CSS via JS after first paint; a `min-height` in header.css alone is too late). **No entrance/pop-in animation on anything in `BaseLayout`/`Header.astro`** — an MPA recreates those elements on every navigation, so the animation replays every time. Desktop search sits flush after the logo, not centered (confirmed 2026-07-09 — don't re-center without asking). The rest of this component's rules — the shared `.logo` sizing, the fixed-height row, why `.user-btn` pre-declares `position:relative` — live in `Header.astro`'s own frontmatter comment.
- **Footer:** ONE minimalist footer only — `Footer.astro` rendered once by `BaseLayout` on every page. Single row: `© year` + platform-name-links-home, then open-a-store (`/seller/register`) / terms / contact / lang-toggle. The old per-store second footer (`.store-platform-note` on store + product pages) was removed 2026-07-27 (two stacked footers + platform name ×3 = clutter) — don't re-add a page-level footer.
- **Data:** Postgres only — `data/*.json` deleted 2026-08-03, `DATABASE_URL` required to boot, everything through `src/lib/db.ts`. The one JSON left is `tests/fixtures/db-data`, which builds the test database.
- **Scroll:** never `window.scrollTo({top,behavior:'smooth'})` for a JS-computed target on this RTL site — native smooth-scroll drifts `scrollX` off 0 mid-animation and can't be corrected. Always `animateScrollTo()` from `scroll-utils.ts`; its header holds the measurement and the three sticky layers a dashboard target must clear.

## Hard rules
- **TypeScript everywhere** — no `.js` in `src/`, no `any`.
- **Tailwind v4 only** — no new CSS files or `<style>` blocks; convert existing CSS **on contact only** (no dedicated conversion sessions — decided 2026-07-15 after 10 rounds showed weak byte-savings ROI; the real payoff is less legacy-CSS context to carry, which only justifies touching a file when a real feature already touches it). **Convert** a plain style on an element the component actually renders — spacing, colour, radius, border, a simple flex/grid box — and **leave the six documented exceptions as CSS; that is not a failure, but say so in the session summary when you skip one**, which is what stops the rule from rotting. **Those six cases with their worked examples, and the inventory of the four files that are still legacy, live in `.claude/hooks/remind-css-conversion.sh` — which re-injects the whole rule the moment you touch one of them, i.e. exactly when it applies.** Two traps that are not in the hook because they bite in new code too: never name a custom class the same as a Tailwind utility (e.g. `.grid`) — unlayered legacy CSS beats `@layer utilities` regardless of source order; and a `@media` override doesn't gain specificity — a same-specificity unscoped rule later in the file wins regardless of viewport, so qualify with an ancestor ID instead. **Also read the `main.css` bullet below before converting**: a per-page `<style>` block already gets its own route chunk, so converting it can *increase* the global bundle.
- **`main.css` is global — every byte loads on every page**, so moving a stylesheet in or out of it is a decision, not a tidy-up. When and when not to split one out, and the counter-intuitive case where converting a page's `<style>` block *increases* the global bundle, are in `main.css`'s own header. Read it before either move.
- **`<Image />` from `astro:assets`** for all platform images.
- **Images optimize themselves — never per-surface, never by reminder (standing user instruction, 2026-07-28).** Every image URL goes through `src/lib/cdn.ts` — `cdnSrc(url,w)` (responsive/hero, pair with `cdnSrcSet` + `sizes`), `cdnThumb(url,w,h)` (any fixed-size cell: cart/order/table/search rows — pass 2x the CSS size), `cdnFill` (off-site consumers only). **Its file header is the rationale** — what a raw URL costs in bytes and in caching, and why Cloudinary *fetch* delivery covers foreign hosts too; read it there, it is not repeated here. Every `<img>` declares `loading` + `decoding`; above-the-fold/LCP images get `loading="eager"` + `fetchpriority="high"`. **Never hand-roll lazy-loading** (IntersectionObserver + `data-lazy-src`): an `<img>` with no `src` is invisible to the browser's preload scanner, so nothing downloads until the page's JS module has run. `tests/image-optimization.test.ts` enforces all of this over `src/**` — fix the markup, never the allowlist. **A skeleton is a BACKDROP, never a cover (2026-07-30):** shimmer under the image (z-index 0, `position:relative;z-index:1` on the img), JS only stops the animation — an on-top `::before` + `opacity:0`-until-JS held 85 already-decoded photos invisible for ~700-820ms on a build. Only an `<img>` with a deferred `data-src`/blank pixel (`.pm-slide`, `#sticky-mini-img-wrap`) may stay JS-driven. Memory `project_skeleton_js_visibility_gate`.
- **Design tokens via `tokens.css`** — never hardcode a hex or a corner radius. Colors: `[color:var(--color-X)]`. **Radius is a 3-step scale** — `--radius-sm` 6px / `--radius` = `--radius-md` 10px (default) / `--radius-lg` 14px, swept across ~215 sites on 2026-07-27; which surface takes which step, and why the scale lives in `@theme` and not `:root`, are documented at the scale itself in `base/tokens.css`. An off-scale `rounded-xl` deliberately fails to compile. Only `lib/email/` is exempt from tokens outright — mail clients don't resolve custom properties.
- **No emoji** — inline SVG icons (`aria-hidden="true"`, `currentColor`).
- **Lighthouse 100 target** — semantic HTML, proper alt/width/height, lazy loading.
- **Mobile-first** — 375px viewport, scale up with `sm:`/`md:`/`lg:`.
- **Modular** — UI = display only; DB/API/Cloudinary → `/src/services/` or `/pages/api/`. No file > 200 lines. SRP always.
- **Dropdown design system** — all dropdowns: `background:var(--color-surface)`, `padding:0.3rem` container, items `var(--radius-sm)` + `padding:0.45rem 0.75rem` + hover `var(--color-bg)`, spring `cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1) 0.13s`.
- **Micro-interactions** — spring `cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1)`, 100–180ms hover / 220–320ms springs; `scale(0.97)` on `:active`, `scale(1.06)` on a card image; no border-darkening, and every animation needs a reason. The per-control recipes (ripple clipping, `.dot-pulse` in-flight, `.btn--confirmed`, `.btn--busy`) are in `components/buttons.css`. **Three standing bans, site-wide:** (1) **native browser dialogs — no `confirm()`, `alert()` or `prompt()` anywhere** (user, 2026-07-28); a confirmation goes through `ConfirmModal.astro`, a notice through `showToast`/`showErrorToast` (`src/lib/toast.ts`) — never hand-build the `toast:show` event, and `grep -n "alert(\|confirm(\|prompt("` over `src/` should stay empty. (2) **the `not-allowed` no-entry cursor** (user, 2026-07-26) — disabled means `cursor:default`, enforced in `reset.css`. (3) **`resize` on a `<textarea>`** — always `resize:none`.
- **One shared badge finish** — every chip in a store card's badge row draws its fill/border/shadow from `.badge-finish` (utilities/utils.css) and differs ONLY by `--badge-hue` — geometry included; the numbers are on the class.
- **Design line — decide by it, not by taste.** Minimalist, timeless, category-agnostic, no gimmicks. Four tests, and a treatment has to pass all four: (1) **Is it already in the system?** Prefer a token, an existing component recipe, or an existing gesture over anything invented for one component — a bespoke treatment reads as imposed even when each individual value is defensible. (2) **Would it date?** Travelling gloss/shine, stacked bevels, decorative diagonals and oversized background glyphs read as a specific year; a surface, a rule and type do not. (3) **Does it suit every category?** The same page must look right for a tool shop, a fashion boutique and a grocery — a coloured frame or a seasonal flourish fails this while a plain surface passes. (4) **Is the colour already spoken for?** Each accent has ONE job (`--color-sale`: the sale price, badge and banner share one green so a shopper learns the mark once); spending it on decoration dilutes the signal it exists to carry. Restraint here IS the design, not an absence of it. The worked example is `.store-sale` in utilities/utils.css — eight bespoke treatments tried and discarded before it became `.card` plus a green number, each failure listed there so the path is not re-walked.
- **A shadow means the surface FLOATS above the page — nothing else earns one** (elevation rule, 2026-08-01/08-02). Dropdown/modal/toast/drawer keeps one at rest. A BAR does not: header and glass filter row are border-only — a value difference or a backdrop-blur already says which layer is in front, and `--shadow-bar` was deleted for want of a consumer. Anything in normal flow — card, tile, hero image, chip, row — is border + `--color-surface`, depth on **hover** only if clickable; pressed = fill + border + weight. A TEXT FIELD focuses by border-colour alone, never a ring. Rationale: `base/tokens.css`, `components/forms.css`; guarded by `design-shadow-rule` + `field-focus-consistency`.
- **Backward-compatible API/DB — always additive (zero-downtime deploy rule).** Old and new versions run against the same DB for a few seconds during a deploy, so every `/api/*` response shape and every schema change must keep the *previous* version working: add fields/columns, never remove or repurpose one the deployed code still reads. A real breaking change is two deploys (stop reading it, then remove it); a breaking API needs `/api/v2/`, not an edit in place. Full deploy-safety context: `GO_LIVE_CHECKLIST.md` §7.
- **Scalability** — stateless API routes; no shared write state. Ask: breaks at 1000 sellers + 10,000 buyers? Schema, indexes, edge cases, measured load: `DB_MIGRATION_PLAN.md`. **Anything the header computes is the cost of the SITE** — it renders on every page a signed-in seller loads, so its indicators (notif badge, avatar alert dot via `sellerHasAnyAlert`) are three multi-store statements no matter how many stores, and must never go back to a per-store loop: a shape the old file cache absorbed is one a ten-connection pool does not. **Independent reads on a click path go in one `Promise.all`** — sequential `await`s are sequential round trips now, and it only actually parallelises through the pool (a single `Client` serialises them, and a measurement written that way will wrongly report "no faster").
- **Inventory integrity** — `decrementStock(productId,qty)` is ONE statement: `UPDATE ... SET stock = stock - qty WHERE id = ? AND stock >= qty`, and the affected-row count IS the answer (0 = reject). No lock, correct on any number of servers — proved 2026-08-03 with 50 concurrent buyers of one unit (`DB_MIGRATION_PLAN.md` §7.5/§9.5; the old process-local mutex is deleted). **A REFUSAL, though, must re-read the count** — the statement's opening snapshot is stale under contention and that number is what the buyer's page clamps to; why, in `store-products.ts#stockAfterRefusal`. Payment webhooks: idempotency check on `paymentRef`.
- **Money & reported numbers — the defence layers are mechanical, don't rebuild them by hand (2026-07-29).** Four modules own the rules and nothing may re-implement them: **`lib/money.ts`** (every amount summed, stored, returned or charged goes through `roundMoney`/`sumMoney`/`percentOf`), **`lib/business-day.ts`** (which day/month an event falls on — `businessDayISO`/`businessMonthKey` for a real instant, `calendarDayISO`/`calendarMonthKey` for a synthetic axis cursor; **confusing the two families IS the bug**, and "use local time" is not the fix — production runs on UTC), **`lib/order-status-rules.ts`** (one row per status, one column per consequence — a new status fills a row, never a new `if` at a call site), **`lib/orders.ts#countsAsRevenue`** (`paymentStatus === 'paid'` alone still matches a *cancelled* order). `tests/money-guards.test.ts` **scans the whole tree**, not a file list, so a brand-new module is covered the moment it exists; its failure message names the helper to use, and adding a file to its allowlist means you just created a second definition of the rule. **A new seller/admin-visible number needs an invariant in `tests/reporting-invariants.test.ts`** (parts sum to whole, two surfaces agree, nothing exceeds its ceiling), plus a case in `tests/reporting-fuzz.test.ts` if weird input can reach it — that is what found the negative-revenue bug nobody had imagined. Every money-moving endpoint needs an idempotency key (`lib/checkout-idempotency.ts`) and a journal entry (`lib/money-events.ts`), enforced by the same scan. Live cross-check of the real data: `lib/reconcile.ts`. Full context: memory `project_metric_integrity_audit`.

Vitest set up (`tests/`, default `node` env, per-file `// @vitest-environment jsdom` only where DOM/localStorage is touched, `npm test`). **Standing policy: any change that moves money or decrements stock needs a Vitest test in the same change** — not "extra scope"; applies to `orders.ts`, `cart.ts`, `/api/checkout`, `store-products(-bulk).ts`, `/api/store-product/bulk`, and any future money/stock code.
